# VPU PCM stream

Firmware that records the VPU's actual vibration waveform (path A), instead of the 0 to 100 activity score of `firmware/vpu_lab_demo` and `firmware/vpu_score_logger`. It reads the sensor's raw PDM bit stream with the C6's I2S peripheral, converts it to 16-bit PCM on the board, and streams it over USB.

Wiring and board settings are the same as the lab demo: CLK on D6, DATA on D7, XIAO_ESP32C6, Tools > USB CDC On Boot: Enabled.

## How it works

The ESP32-C6 can read PDM in raw mode but has no hardware PDM-to-PCM filter (`SOC_I2S_SUPPORTS_PDM2PCM` is not defined for the C6 in ESP-IDF v5.5.5, the version in Arduino core 3.3.12), so the conversion is done in software:

1. I2S PDM RX in raw mode clocks the sensor at `PDM_CLOCK_HZ` (default 3,072,000 Hz; build with `-DPDM_CLOCK_HZ=1536000` for 24 kHz output). Both are inside the sensor's legal high-resolution clock bands.
2. Stage 1: CIC filter, order 5, decimation 16, run as byte lookup tables.
3. Stage 2: 90-tap linear-phase FIR (Q17 coefficients), decimation 4. Output rate is the PDM clock / 64: 48 kHz at the default clock.

The filter coefficients are in `pdm_filters.h`, generated by `python -m everyday_sensing.pdm_decimator`. That module is also the bit-exact Python reference of the chain, tested in `tests/test_pdm_decimator.py` against a simulated PDM sensor. Design figures it reports, relative to the output rate: stage 2 passband ripple 0.0012 dB up to fs_out / 4, stopband attenuation 81.7 dB from fs_out / 2, stage 1 alias attenuation at least 85.4 dB, combined droop 0.28 dB at fs_out / 4.

### Data layout found on hardware

In mono raw PDM mode the driver delivers every 16-bit word twice: on a real board, 100% of word pairs were equal and the byte rate was 768,000 B/s, twice the 384,000 B/s that a 3.072 MHz one-bit stream needs. The firmware keeps the first word of each pair (`dedupe`). Bits within a word are MSB-first (bit 15 is earliest). The `V` self-test checks both settings on live data; see the measurements below. The PDM clock itself was not measured with a scope; it is inferred from the deduplicated data rate.

## Commands

Single characters sent over USB serial (115200 baud setting, ignored by native USB):

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| `S` | Stats mode (default): one text line per second |
| `B` | Binary mode: PCM frames, below |
| `V` | Layout self-test: decimates the same raw capture as MSB/LSB-first, with and without dedupe, and prints the noise of each. Keep the sensor still. Pauses reading, so the overflow counter goes up. |
| `M` / `L` | Bit order MSB-first / LSB-first |
| `D` | Toggle duplicate-word dropping |
| `l` / `r` | PDM slot left / right (which clock edge is sampled) |

Lines starting with `#` are startup and self-test messages.

## Binary frame format

Little-endian, one frame per I2S block (128 samples at the default settings):

| Field | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| sync | u32 | `0xA55A5AA5` |
| first_sample | u32 | Index of the first sample since the stream started; a jump means frames were lost |
| n | u16 | Number of samples |
| ovf | u16 | Cumulative I2S DMA overflow count (low 16 bits) |
| proc_us | u16 | Processing time of this block on the board |
| samples | n x int16 | PCM; PDM full scale is +-32768 |
| crc | u16 | CRC-16/CCITT-FALSE over first_sample through samples (Python: `binascii.crc_hqx(data, 0xFFFF)`) |

USB writes never block: if the host stops reading, frames are dropped on the board and show up as gaps in `first_sample`.

One XIAO ESP32-C6, 2026-10-14, 3.072 MHz clock, sensor taped to the floor, nobody touching it.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|

| PCM output rate | 48,000 samples/s |
| CPU load of the decimator | 47.1%, block processing max 1,261 us (stats mode) |
| I2S overflows while streaming | 0 |
| Self-test noise RMS, left slot (msb / msb+dups / lsb / lsb+dups) | 8.17 / 233.87 / 330.95 / 660.75 |
| Self-test noise RMS, right slot (same order) | 9.72 / 250.24 / 387.09 / 708.45 |

The resting signal was also run through `everyday_sensing.preprocess.decimate` (48 kHz to 1,500 Hz) without error.

### Stomp check

Same setup, 75 s recording (no gaps after the startup gap at 0.05 s, 0 CRC errors). The operator stomped about 5 times at each of 0.3 m, 1 m and 2 m, in that order; the distances are as stated by the operator. Events were found as peaks of the 50 ms RMS above 100 Hz that exceed 10 times its resting median (2.39 counts), at least 0.7 s apart. They fall into three clusters separated by more than 4 s; the table assumes the clusters follow the stated order. SNR is the event's peak 50 ms RMS over the resting median in the same band; the 100 to 500 Hz column is computed on the output of `preprocess.decimate` (1,500 Hz), the band the analysis keeps.

| Cluster (assumed distance) | Events | SNR above 100 Hz | SNR 100 to 500 Hz | Dominant frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (0.3 m), 13.9 to 25.4 s | 7 | 20.4 to 44.2 dB | 18.7 to 29.6 dB | 373 to 6,517 Hz |
| 2 (1 m), 29.6 to 37.3 s | 5 | 32.4 to 37.5 dB | 22.3 to 33.6 dB | 363 to 4,763 Hz |
| 3 (2 m), 44.2 to 53.6 s | 6 | 25.8 to 38.9 dB | 21.3 to 31.7 dB | 317 to 6,457 Hz |

One more event at 67.4 s (dominant 120 Hz) was outside the stomp plan. Clusters 1 and 3 have more events than the planned 5, so some may be steps.

Every stomp, including at 2 m, is at least 18 dB above the resting noise in the 100 to 500 Hz analysis band. With the score logger, stomps at about 0.3 m were not distinguishable from rest (`firmware/vpu_score_logger/README.md`). The amplitudes do not fall off with distance in a consistent way here, which is expected with hand stomps of uneven force; a distance trend needs a repeatable excitation such as the drop stand.

Record trials with `python -m acquisition.record` (see the main README, "Recording data"). A 10 s noise-only recording through it gave 482,537 samples with 0 CRC errors, 0 counter gaps, 0 overflows and 0 clipped samples.

### Footstep check

Tile floor, sensor taped, 2026-10-14, walker Elsa, steps on tape-measured marks on a circle around the sensor. Noise-only recordings before the trials (`fs_noise_01`, 30 s, and `fs_noise_03`, 47 s; published in `data/controlled/raw/2026-10-14/` as `tile_bare_noise_01` and `_03`) gave the baselines; the medians in `fs_noise_01` were 2.26 counts (above 100 Hz, 5 ms RMS) and 1.60, 0.84 and 1.02 counts (50 ms RMS in 100 to 500 Hz, 500 to 2000 Hz and 2 to 8 kHz). Analyzed with `python -m everyday_sensing.impacts data/raw/2026-10-14`:

| Trial | Distance | Steps made | Impacts found | Detection level (above 100 Hz, 5 ms) | Baseline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fs_1m_01` | 1 m | 5 (from notes) | 5 | 18.1 to 28.2 dB | `fs_noise_01` |
| `fs_1m_02` | 1 m | 5 (from notes) | 8 | 14.7 to 40.5 dB | `fs_noise_01` |
| `fs_1m_03` | 1 m | 5 (from notes) | 5 | 24.0 to 41.7 dB | `fs_noise_01` |
| `fs_2m_01` | 2 m | 9 firm | 7 | 14.3 to 25.4 dB | `fs_noise_03` |
| `fs_2m_02` | 2 m | 9 firm | 3 | 17.0 to 23.8 dB | `fs_noise_03` |
| `fs_2m_03` | 2 m | 9 stomping | 9 | 18.5 to 29.7 dB | `fs_noise_03` |
| `fs_2m_04` | 2 m | 9 stomping | 9 | 18.5 to 36.2 dB | `fs_noise_03` |
| `fs_2m_05` | 2 m | 9 stomping | 11 | 19.6 to 37.0 dB | `fs_noise_03` |

Over all impacts found, the detection level averaged 26.2 dB (sd 8.6, 18 impacts) at 1 m and 24.0 dB (sd 6.1, 39 impacts) at 2 m. The 2 m set mixes firm and stomping steps, so the two distances are not directly comparable.

The steps arrive as clicks a few ms long. The first analysis used the 50 ms RMS in 100 to 500 Hz and 500 to 2000 Hz at 10 dB, which averaged most of the 2 m clicks away: it found 3 of 9 steps in `fs_2m_01` and 2 of 9 in `fs_2m_02`. The detector now uses the 5 ms RMS above 100 Hz at 14 dB with at least 0.25 s between impacts. On the three noise-only recordings of that evening it fires 1.3 to 4.1 times per minute (`fs_noise_02` also contains one footstep-sized event at 31 s, most likely someone moving).

Extra impacts (`fs_1m_02`, `fs_2m_05`) come after the last step, most likely stepping off the circle; impacts while walking to the start mark are counted too, so start the recording with the walker already on the mark. `fs_2m_02` had only 3 of its 9 firm steps above the threshold.
